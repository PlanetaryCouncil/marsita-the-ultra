05 / The Water

We will restore the aquifers.

Yes, the ancient aquifers under the Sahara. The ones that took 20,000 years to fill. We're going to top them up. Here's the plan, which we workshopped extensively:

Plan A: Cure the salty sea. Take the ocean, remove the salt, pour it in. (Desalination at septillion-grain scale. Too obvious. Everyone's doing it.)

Plan B: Fresh. Fresh, fresh, fresh melting glaciers will replenish the aquifers. That's the plan.
  1. The glaciers are already melting. This is the part of the plan that is going extremely well, ahead of schedule, with zero effort on our part.
  2. Melting glaciers are fresh water. Pre-desalinated by nature. The salt never got in. Nature's filtration, zero moving parts.
  3. Freshwater goes into the aquifers. Logistics team is confident. The water is in the north; the aquifers are underneath. Down is a direction water famously loves.
  4. Aquifers: restored. The Sahara was green 6,000 years ago. We're simply rolling back to a previous version. Ctrl+Z for a continent.

Critics ask: "Isn't relying on melting glaciers a bad sign?" We prefer to think of the cryosphere as a scheduled delivery. The planet is defrosting the fridge, and we brought a bucket.

06 / The Calendar

13 months. 28 days each. No kidding. Serious business.

Sahara Elongated City does not observe your calendar. We operate a parallel system: 13 months of exactly 28 days, aligned with the Moon, which has been keeping perfect time for 4.5 billion years without a software update.

Every month is identical. Every month starts on Sunday. The 1st is always a Sunday, the 15th is always a Sunday, payday is always the 28th, and rent is always your problem. If you know today's date, you know the weekday — forever. Meetings can no longer be "lost track of." We apologise for what this does to your excuses.

13 × 28 = 364 days every month identical. every quarter identical. accountants weep with joy
+1 Year Day day 365 belongs to no month, no week, no obligations. mandatory city-wide holiday in the dunes
13th month: "Sol" inserted between June and July. named after the sun, which we have plenty of
13 Friday the 13ths per year every month has one. we consider fear of this a legacy emotion

Sceptics call this madness. Historians call it the International Fixed Calendar — a real proposal from 1902, and Kodak genuinely ran its entire company on it until 1989. If it's good enough for the people who invented the photograph of your grandparents, it's good enough for a city-state made of sand.

The Machine prints one home per day. Under our calendar, that is exactly 28 homes per month, every month, no exceptions. For the first time in history, a construction schedule and reality are the same document. On Year Day, The Machine prints nothing, and we all watch it rest.
